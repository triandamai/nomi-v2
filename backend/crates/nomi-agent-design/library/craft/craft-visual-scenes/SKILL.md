# Shader, 3D and generative visuals

When a page may carry a shader ground, a 3D object or a generative pattern, how to choose one with a real job, and how to draw its still on a canvas that has only solid fills and paths.

open-design lists these three as catalogue entries only (a one-line description and a link to the upstream code skill), so there was no design guidance to port; this skill is written from the topics they name, under Taste's rules.

## Where they are allowed

Only on software, gaming and tech hardware pages, as the page's single effect or big scene (`taste-core`, `taste-photos-motion`). Never on a cafe, bakery, guesthouse, salon, shop, service firm, agency or studio: those get photographs. An effect never stands where the product, the device or the game's world should be.

## The three families

| Family | What it is | Good jobs | Wrong jobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shader ground** (GLSL) | A live, computed surface: soft noise, flowing fields, light falloff, ray-marched forms | A calm living ground behind the app window in a software hero; a "signal" field for an AI or network product | Decorating text, filling an empty section, replacing a product shot |
| **3D scene** (Three.js, WebGL) | A real-time object or space with materials, light and camera | The actual hardware product turning to show its ports; a game's world; a data globe for a logistics tool | A floating abstract blob, a 3D logo spinning, a render when the brief has photos (Taste: render only if the brief asks for 3D) |
| **Generative art** (seeded, p5-style) | Patterns drawn by rules from a seed, the same every render | A pattern derived from the product's data (each user's order history drawn as a unique mark); a brand texture for a creative tool; still posters | Random noise for atmosphere on a page with nothing to say |

## Choosing one: four questions

1. **What does it show that a photo or the UI can't?** Movement of data, inner structure, the product from every side. If the answer is "mood", use the product instead.
2. **What is its single idea?** "Orders flowing into one inbox" is an idea; "dynamic gradient" is not.
3. **Where does it sit?** In the hero ground behind the h1 (calm area under the text), in one framed visual under an h2, or in its own two-screen section. Never two effects on the page.
4. **How does it rest?** It must look finished when still, because phones, reduced motion and slow devices see the still.

## Briefing it (the note for Koda)

`Effect · <name> · <section and placement> · idea: <one line> · palette: <tokens> · motion: <speed, loop length> · interaction: <none / pointer / scroll> · still: <frame id> · pause control if over 5 s · stops off screen · reduced: still`

Example: `Effect · signal-field · hero ground behind app window, calm area left under h1 · idea: messages from WhatsApp, Shopee and Instagram drift into one line · palette: $color-background, $color-primary at 20%, $color-chart-2 · motion: slow drift, 12 s loop · interaction: none · still: hero-effect-poster · pause button bottom right · reduced: still`.

Keep it slow and low in contrast behind text; text over a live ground still needs Taste's contrast at every frame.

## Drawing the still

The canvas has solid fills, strokes, shadows and paths: no gradients, blur, images or shaders. Build the poster from those:
- **Fields and noise:** layered shapes in two or three close tones of the palette (a darker ground, mid-tone curved bands as paths, a few lighter ellipses for light), all flat. Fewer, larger shapes read better than many tiny ones.
- **Flow lines:** many thin stroked paths following one direction, one stroke width, varying only in tone.
- **Particles and points:** small ellipses on a loose grid, sized in two or three steps; a cluster where the idea concentrates (where the messages meet).
- **3D object:** a photo placeholder at the final size with the note naming the render: `Render · keyboard three-quarter view from the left, matte black, soft top light, on $color-background`. Don't attempt a vector imitation of a 3D render.
- **Generative pattern:** draw a real sample of the rule with paths: a grid of 6 x 6 marks generated by the rule you describe, so the person sees the system, not a texture.

Name the frame `<section>-effect-poster` and keep its element count low (the preview has a 300-element limit; a poster should take 30 or fewer).

## Quality bar

- One idea, readable in the still.
- Palette from the page's tokens; the effect is part of the system, not a separate artwork.
- Calm behind text, a clear area where the h1 sits.
- Seeded and deterministic: the same page always shows the same pattern, so screenshots and the person's feedback match what they see.
- Lightweight: Koda will pause it off screen and cap its frame rate on phones; design so a lower-detail version still looks right.
