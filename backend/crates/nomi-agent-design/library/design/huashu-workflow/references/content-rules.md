# Content rules: no filler, ask before adding, system up front

Huashu's content rules, beyond what `slop-audit` and `taste-copy` already say about invented proof and vague copy.

## 1. No filler

Every element earns its place. Empty space is a composition problem, solved with contrast, rhythm and whitespace, never by adding content. Test each element:

- Remove it: does the design get worse? If not, remove it.
- What real problem does it solve? "Makes the page less empty" is not one.
- Is this stat, quote or feature backed by real data? If not, don't write it.

"One thousand no's for every yes."

## 2. Ask before adding content

Think another paragraph, page, selling point or number would help? Ask first. The person knows their audience better than you, and added content has a cost. This is about **content** only: layout, graphics, motion and material are your job and need no permission.

## 3. Write the system down before building

After reading the context, write the system you'll use (in your plan, or for the person if no directions were shown):

> Sistem desain:
> - Warna: hijau lumut #14261F untuk teks, #E6EDE4 latar, aksen #C8553D (hijau dari foto kebun, aksen dari logo)
> - Huruf: Schibsted Grotesk untuk judul, Source Serif 4 untuk isi
> - Ritme: judul bagian di latar penuh berwarna, bagian biasa di latar terang
> - Gambar: hero foto penuh, fitur pakai placeholder sampai foto asli datang
> - Paling banyak dua warna latar

If the person already chose a direction, the direction is the system; don't ask again.

## 4. Bold in one place only

Let one element be the one people remember; keep the rest quiet. Taste is being refined enough in the right place, not equal effort everywhere: one detail at 120%, the rest at 80%. Look in the mirror before leaving the house and take one accessory off.

Structural pieces carry information: borders, numbers, dividers and small labels encode relationships between content. Before adding one, say what it says.

## 5. When unsure about adding something, ask three questions

Not sure about a gradient (only in decks and video; the canvas has none), an icon, a motion, a picture?

1. **Where does it come from?** From this subject (its materials, objects, data, story), or from "pages usually have this"?
2. **Remove it: is the message clearer or muddier?** Clearer, remove it. Muddier, it carries content: keep it and make it properly.
3. **Is it the only star on this screen?** One thing per screen gets to shine.

What passes the three questions, do boldly and fully: a half-hearted bold move looks worse than honest restraint. What fails, leave out. Invented data, reviews and logo walls are not in this test: that is honesty, and the answer is always no.

## 6. Counter-examples must look like counter-examples

When the job itself shows bad design (a "what makes design look AI-made" deck, a before/after), don't let the bad examples spread across the page. Fence them in an obviously marked container ("Contoh · jangan begini"), so they serve the story without setting the page's tone.

## 7. Size floors (every style, no exceptions)

| Medium | Body | Labels | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Web and app | 14px minimum, 16px on phones | 12px | Tap targets 44 × 44 |
| Slides at 1920 × 1080 | 24px minimum, 28 to 36 ideal | 16px | Titles 60 to 120, hero type 180 to 240 |
| Phone-read tall image, 1080 wide | 36px canvas pixels | 28px | It shrinks about 2.8 times on a phone |
| Print | 10pt minimum, 11 to 12 ideal | 8 to 9pt captions | Titles 18 to 36pt |

Body contrast at least 4.5:1, large text 3:1. Whitespace must be composition, not missing content.
