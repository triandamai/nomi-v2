# Direction cards: template and example

Write six cards before you build anything. Each card is one short paragraph or a row in a table, specific enough that a reader can picture the first screen.

## The fields

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Logic and source | Which designer, which work (with where you saw it), or which roulette entry |
| What is borrowed | The one mechanism, in a sentence without the source's looks, plus the numbers you measured: grid, size ratios, area shares, picture to text relation, the shape of the data |
| Not borrowed, grown instead | The source's expression you refuse this time (its palette, type temperament, shape language), and where this design's colour, type and shapes come from in the subject |
| Hero material | What carries the screen: real photo, generated image, data drawn as image, big type, a recreated object (ticket, label, menu card), a drawn interface |
| Skeleton | The layout structure in one sentence |
| Where the boldness goes | The single boldest place; everything else serves the content |
| Where the form comes from | One sentence: what in this design exists only because of this subject |

## Example: website for a third-generation kecap (soy sauce) maker in Tegal

- **Best designer · Kenya Hara.** Borrowed: huge whitespace, the material itself as hero, very few words. Hero: real photos of clay jars and the drying yard. Skeleton: one screen, one picture, one sentence, a slow vertical rhythm. Boldness: the first screen is one full close-up of salt crust. Form: kecap dries in the sun for months, so the page is slow.
- **Real reference · an award-winning Japanese heritage food site (screenshot in notes).** Borrowed: a vertical title column beside horizontal body text (title column 18% of width); the whole site in two colours, the second under 10% of area. Not borrowed: its vermilion, washi paper ground and Mincho type; colours here come from the jar glaze and the salt bloom, type is a sturdy serif. Hero: bottle product shot plus the tall title. Skeleton: tall title left, three content columns right. Boldness: only two colours. Form: the "family recipe" character of an old brand.
- **Seconds roulette · Editorial Brutalism.** Borrowed: extreme size contrast and a modular grid. Hero: the word "KECAP" cut huge into the first screen. Skeleton: modular grid, picture and text alternating. Boldness: the size contrast on screen one. Form: one word tells a whole factory.

For a site, every card still obeys Taste: the palette and pairing on each card are dealt from `taste-color` and `taste-type` for the kind (a product brand here), and the house rules (one section per screen, real photos, equal edges) bind all three.

## Example: a 20-second promo video for a laundry app

- **Best designer · Saul Bass.** Borrowed: cut-paper shapes and one strong diagonal per shot. Hero: a shirt silhouette folding itself. Boldness: the fold that becomes the logo.
- **Real reference · an app's store preview video (seen).** Borrowed: one continuous screen recording, one caption per feature, only part of the interface visible at a time. Not borrowed: its blue and its rounded sans; colour from the app's own tokens. Hero: the order screen.
- **Roulette · Black Big-Number Stage.** Borrowed: one huge number per shot. Hero: "2 jam" (two hours) slamming in. Form: the promise is speed.

## Presenting

Show the three drafts side by side with a three-line summary each, then the alternates:
