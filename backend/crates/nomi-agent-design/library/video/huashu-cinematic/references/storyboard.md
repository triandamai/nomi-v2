# Storyboard: every shot is first a cover that moves

The storyboard is never skipped, even for a five-second animation: skipping it gives you timetable thinking (what appears at which second) instead of picture thinking (what this frame looks like). Under 20 seconds: the light storyboard card below, one row per shot. 20 seconds or more: the card is the minimum of the director's notes. Launch films: the full notes (`SKILL.md` §8).

A good cover is **one concrete hero, at most three active elements, one clear line for the eye.** Pause the finished video on any frame: it should work as a cover. So: design the still frames first, then the motion.

## The eleven frame laws

1. **Cover test:** each scene's frame has one concrete hero, at most three active elements, one focal point. "At most three" holds for every frame: when something enters, something leaves (fades, steps back, blurs and dims).
2. **A push-in ends on a concrete anchor:** a logo, a key number, that button, that line, a face. Never texture or decoration. Name the anchor on the card or delete the move.
3. **The eye line is the camera script.** The guiding line in the still frame (a gaze, the one arrow, the reading direction) is where the camera goes. One guiding line per scene; none means fix the composition, not invent a move.
4. **Words and picture on two tracks:** big type 2 to 6 words (2 to 4 best) carries the hook, the picture carries the mood. Type enters in two beats: the action word slams in first, the qualifier follows on a colour block ("Pesan" lands, "dalam 2 menit" sticks on). Type sits in planned negative space, never on the hero. Solid strong colours; never hollow white type with a coloured outline.
5. **Composition in percentages:** "app screen top right 60% in perspective, person bottom left 25%, big type on the screenshot's top edge, 10% safe area." At least three percentages per shot, or it's literature, not a spec.
6. **Foreground, midground, background** named, with who covers whom: that is the parallax.
7. **Setup and payoff:** each scene's opening frame leaves a gap (an unfinished bridge, a number not yet revealed, half the frame empty) that the motion closes. A frame that shows everything and then wiggles is a moving poster.
8. **Make the abstract physical, with a process:** "connecting" is a bridge built piece by piece; "price cut" is Rp 89.000 struck through to Rp 29.000; "too AI-looking" is grey squares filling a page in neat stagger. If the image is only a noun, it has no process; find a verb.
9. **Style and composition are orthogonal:** the skin (palette, type, material) is set once for the film; the composition template changes every scene. Any two shots: same style, clearly different composition.
10. **Inherited no-go zone:** glowing particles, data streams, holographic HUDs and neon are motion graphics' laziest crutches. Tech feels real through real UI, clean big type and light grounds. If anything glows, justify it.
11. **Three-metre test:** the biggest word in any frame is readable from three metres; don't shrink type "because it moves". Shrink a keyframe to phone size and read it.

## Shot sizes as zoom tiers

| Size | Zoom | Shows | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme wide | 0.78× | Everything plus space | Opening, closing group shot |
| Wide | 1× (establishing 1.06×) | The whole interface or scene | Home base for most shots |
| Medium | 1.3 to 1.45× | One feature area | The workhorse of feature demos |
| Close | 1.8× | One component, one data point | A specific interaction |
| Extreme close | 2.3× (maximum) | A law-2 anchor | Key number, that button, the logo |

No two neighbouring shots at the same size; no jump of two sizes (wide straight to extreme close) unless it's a deliberate punch-in covered by a flash or whip.

## The energy arc for films of 10 seconds or more

| Part | Share | Energy | Content | Hard rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | 8 to 12% | Low | Brand or topic | Wordmark hold 1s or more |
| Hero portrait | 12 to 15% | Low-mid, the slowest | One hero's complete arc (enter, hover, settle) | Arc 3s or more, the highest craft |
| Feature climb | 55 to 65% | Alternating high and low | One unique feature per shot | After every one or two feature shots, a breathing card (2 to 6 words, big space) |
| Close | 13 to 16% | The film's peak | Group shot and sign-off | Hold; end wide |

**Reserve still time first, then place motion:** list features and count shots; subtract the untouchable stills (wordmark hold, 0.5s after batch motion, the 3s opening arc, the 0.8s closing wide, 0.5s before key results); give the rest to motion, never two high-energy shots in a row; choose each seam's transition from the shots' own time. Slow-Fast-Boom-Stop shapes single short pieces (15s or less); the arc shapes multi-shot films.

## The light storyboard card (eight fields per shot)

| # | Time | Size | Camera + motive | Composition (percentages, layers) | Key motion (one per shot) | Transition to next | Acceptance frames |

The camera field includes "still, because…" when still; every push names its anchor. The transition field is never empty or "cut". Acceptance frames: one or two frame numbers per shot, written **before** building, checked after.

Example, 12 seconds at 30fps, a stock app for a warung ("StokWarung"):

| # | Time | Size | Camera | Composition | Motion | Transition | Frames |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## The thumbnail pass (before building)

Make a grey-box version of three to five key frames (opening setup, hero frame, one climb frame, peak, close): flat grey blocks and labels in the card's percentages, no fonts, no colour, no shadows (styling is the direction's job; styling now means avoiding the composition problem). Put them side by side and ask: with labels covered, are the compositions clearly different? Does each pass the cover test? Can you point to each one's guiding line? Only then build. The direction board is the first thumbnail; the pass extends it into the film's keyframe sequence.

## Choosing transitions by narrative relation

| Relation between shots | First choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Time jump ("tiga hari kemudian") | Title card or flash wash | Whip pan |
| Same interface, different area | **Don't cut: one pan** | Hidden cut |
| Contrast (before and after) | Mask wipe | Split, then a cut with a white flash |
| Cause and effect | Shared element becomes the next hero | Morph |
| Big energy drop (breathing card to a high shot) | Flash wash | Whip pan |
| Small energy drop (neighbouring features) | Defocus hand-off or a cross-fade of 8 frames or more | Hidden cut |

Done when: the card exists with all eight fields; frames checked against laws 1, 3 and 5 at least; no same-size neighbours or two-size jumps; stills reserved and breathing cards placed; every seam has a transition; the thumbnail pass is answered; acceptance frames are written.
