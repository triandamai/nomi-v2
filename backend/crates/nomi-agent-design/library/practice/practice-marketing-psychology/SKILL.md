# Practice: marketing psychology

The mental models behind why people buy and act, each translated into an honest design or copy move on the canvas, with the manipulative ones marked as not for you.

Use a model to explain a choice you already have a reason for, never to trick a visitor. Every move here must stay true to the brief: Taste's rules on invented proof, placeholders and dark patterns win over any persuasion idea. For each request: name the behaviour you want, what the visitor believes before they arrive, where they are (just found you, comparing, ready to buy) and what stops them today.

## Thinking models (for your plan, not for the page)

| Model | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| First principles / 5 whys | Don't add a blog, a chatbot or a testimonial wall because competitors have one. Ask why the visitor needs it |
| Jobs to be done | People hire a product for a job. A cake buyer's job is "a birthday that goes well", not "a 20 cm cake": the hero shows the cake on the table, the copy names the date and pickup |
| Inversion | List what would guarantee failure (h1 nobody understands, the order button below three screens, no price, no hours) and design each out |
| Occam's razor | When a page "doesn't convert", check the obvious first: the button, the price, the form length, the phone layout |
| Pareto (80/20) | The few things that drive most orders (menu and price, booking, location) get the biggest, clearest space |
| Theory of constraints | Fix the one bottleneck first: a perfect hero is wasted if the booking form has 12 fields |
| Second-order thinking | A permanent "diskon 50%" banner sells today and trains buyers to wait for discounts |
| Map is not territory | A persona is a model; the owner's real WhatsApp chats show real buyers |
| Curse of knowledge | What is obvious to the owner ("paket A", "sistem PO") confuses a newcomer. Spell it out |
| Fundamental attribution error | Visitors who leave aren't "not serious"; the page or form is usually the cause |

## How buyers decide: honest moves

| Model | Honest move on the canvas |
|---|---|
| Mere exposure | One consistent look, name and main-action wording everywhere (nav, hero, close, phone bar): familiarity builds trust |
| Availability | Make the good outcome easy to picture: the real dish, the room as booked, the app screen with real data |
| Confirmation bias | Start from what the visitor already believes ("roti sourdough harus asam") and add the fact, rather than fighting them head-on |
| Endowment | A free trial, sample or free plan lets them "own" it first; show the trial length plainly |
| IKEA effect | Let them configure: pick cake size and writing, build a hampers box, choose a package. Show the choice they made in the summary |
| Zero price | "Gratis ongkir di Sleman" or "Coba gratis 14 hari" pulls harder than a small discount, when it's true |
| Present bias | Lead with today's benefit ("Nota terkirim begitu pesanan masuk") over a future one ("ROI dalam 6 bulan") |
| Status quo bias | Make switching feel safe and small: "Impor daftar menu dari Excel", "Pindah tanpa ganti nomor WhatsApp" (only if true) |
| Default effect | Pre-select the option most people really need (the usual pickup time, the monthly plan for a first buyer). Never pre-tick add-ons, newsletter consent or auto-renew |
| Paradox of choice / Hick's law | Fewer options, faster decisions: one main action a screen, two or three plans, short forms, a menu grouped into few categories |
| Goal gradient | Show progress in multi-step flows: "Langkah 2 dari 3", a filled bar on onboarding |
| Peak-end rule | Design the best moment and the ending: the confirmation screen names what was ordered and when it's ready, in the button's words |
| Zeigarnik | An unfinished profile or a saved cart shows what's left ("Tinggal tambah alamat"). Never nag |
| Pratfall effect | Admitting a real limit builds trust: "Kami tidak buka hari Senin", "Belum bisa kirim ke luar Jawa" |
| Mental accounting | Frame a price in the unit the buyer thinks in, with the billed amount still clear: "Rp 8.300 per hari, ditagih Rp 249.000 per bulan" |
| Regret aversion | Reduce fear of a wrong choice with real terms beside the button: "Batal gratis sampai H-2", "Bisa ganti paket kapan saja" |
| Social proof | Only real proof from the brief: actual review count and quote with the client's permission, real client logos. None given: a strip of plain facts or nothing (Taste wins) |

## Influence: honest and not

| Model | Honest use | Not for you |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Give real value first: the full price list, a free size guide, a useful FAQ, a free plan | Gating basic info (prices, hours) behind a sign-up |
| Commitment and consistency | Small first step: "Cek jadwal kosong" before "Booking", a free trial before paying | Tricking a small yes into a bigger obligation |
| Authority | Real credentials from the brief: a licence, certification, a press mention, the chef's real training | Invented badges, "Featured in" logos, "rekomendasi dokter" without proof |
| Liking / similarity | The owner's real photo and story, local words the customers use | Invented founder stories or stock people posed as staff |
| Unity | Speak as part of the community the business really serves ("warga Kotagede") | Fake insider language |
| Scarcity / urgency | Real limits stated plainly: "PO ditutup Jumat 17.00", "6 kamar" | Fake countdowns, "tinggal 2!" without real stock, timers that reset (Taste bans popups and badges too) |
| Foot in the door | A ladder of real steps: free trial, monthly, yearly | Hidden upgrades |
| Door in the face | | Showing an absurd price first so the real one feels cheap: not for you |
| Loss aversion | Name what really happens: "Data Anda tetap tersimpan 30 hari setelah masa coba habis" | Guilt or fear copy: "Jangan sampai menyesal", "Anda akan kehilangan pelanggan" |
| Anchoring | Show the real reference: the plan above it, a real former price with its date, the cost of the old way in the client's own numbers | Inflated "harga coret" that never existed |
| Decoy effect | | A deliberately bad plan added to push another: not for you. Every plan must be one a real buyer would choose |
| Framing | Lead with the positive true frame: "98 dari 100 pesanan diantar tepat waktu" when that's the real figure | Frames that hide the cost or the condition |
| Contrast | Show the before state honestly: the stack of paper notes beside the app | Doctored or exaggerated "before" pictures |

## Pricing psychology (you show prices, you don't set them)

Prices come from the brief; never change them. You decide only how they're presented.
- **Left-digit effect:** Rp 49.900 reads cheaper than Rp 50.000. If the owner already uses such prices, show them exactly; don't suggest rewriting a whole menu.
- **Round prices** read premium and easy (Rp 500.000 for a wedding cake consult). Taste's rule for assumed prices (uneven and local, Rp 28.000) applies only when you're told to assume.
- **Rule of 100:** under Rp 100.000 a percent discount looks bigger ("hemat 20%"); above it an amount looks bigger ("hemat Rp 100.000"). Use whichever form the owner's real offer takes.
- **Good-better-best:** three tiers with the middle as the usual choice works because buyers judge relative to neighbours; mark the recommended one for a stated reason (`pattern-landing-pricing`), never by reflex.
- **Per-day framing:** "Rp 8.300 per hari" makes a subscription feel smaller; the billed amount and period stay visible beside it.

## Design and behaviour models

| Model | On the canvas |
|---|---|
| Fogg: Behaviour = Motivation x Ability x Prompt | Motivation: the picture and the promise. Ability: a short form, a big tap target, WhatsApp prefilled. Prompt: the main button where the decision happens. Missing one, nothing happens |
| EAST: Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely | Easy path, real photos, real proof if any, the ask at the moment they're ready (after the menu, not before) |
| COM-B: Capability, Opportunity, Motivation | Can they do it on a phone? Is the path visible? Do they want to? |
| Activation energy | Make the first step tiny: one field, a date picker already open, a prefilled message |
| AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | The page order as a check, not a template (Taste: shape follows the reader's questions) |
| Rule of 7 | One page won't convert everyone on first visit; consistent name, look and wording across Instagram, Maps and the site help the later visits |
| Cobra effect | A referral reward can attract fake referrals; design the terms with the owner |

## Quick reference

| Problem | Models to reach for |
|---|---|
| Visitors don't act | Hick's law, activation energy, Fogg, theory of constraints |
| "Too expensive" | Anchoring (real), framing, mental accounting, regret aversion |
| No trust yet | Authority (real), pratfall, reciprocity, social proof from the brief |
| Can't decide | Paradox of choice, default (honest), good-better-best |
| Drop-off in onboarding | Goal gradient, IKEA effect, commitment, activation energy |

## Never

Fake countdowns or stock, fake "orang lain sedang melihat ini", pre-ticked consent or add-ons, decoy plans, guilt copy on the "no" button ("Tidak, saya tidak mau untung"), hidden close buttons, prices that appear only at the last step. If a person asks for one, say plainly that it costs trust and offer the honest version.
