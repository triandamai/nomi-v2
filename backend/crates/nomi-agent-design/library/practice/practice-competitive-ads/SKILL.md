# Reading the competition

Read the ads and pages the person's competitors run, find what repeats and what's missing, and use it to make the person's own design say something the others don't.

You don't scrape ad libraries. The person sends what they see: screenshots of Instagram or TikTok ads, Meta Ad Library pages, marketplace listings (Shopee, Tokopedia), Google Maps profiles, competitor websites. Work from those.

## What to pull out of each example

| Look for | Example (a bakery in Yogyakarta) |
|---|---|
| The problem it names | "Kue ultah dadakan? Bisa hari ini." (last-minute cakes) |
| The buyer it speaks to | parents planning a child's party; office admins ordering snacks |
| The promise | same-day, halal, free delivery in town |
| The proof it uses | photo of the shop, number of branches, marketplace rating |
| The action | "Pesan via WhatsApp", "Order di GoFood" |
| The visual pattern | product top-down on a white ground; a person holding the box; before/after |
| Format and length | single photo with short text, short video, carousel post |

Then group the examples by theme ("pengiriman cepat", "harga", "halal", "custom desain") and count how often each appears.

## What to conclude

1. **The crowded claims.** If five competitors all say "enak dan murah", saying it too makes the person invisible.
2. **The empty spot.** A real fact the person has that nobody leads with: open from 06.00, the only one with gluten-free, the owner bakes on site.
3. **What buyers are shown.** If every strong ad shows the real product big, that is a signal for the hero; if all of them show stock smiles, real photos will stand out.
4. **The action buyers expect.** If everyone orders through WhatsApp, a web checkout needs a strong reason; match the expected path or explain the better one.
5. **Their tone,** so the person's voice can differ on purpose.

Write it as a short note to the person: three crowded claims, one or two open positions, and what that means for the h1, the hero picture and the main action.

## Turn it into design decisions

- The h1 and first-screen fact come from the open position, stated as a plain fact (`taste-copy`), never as a jab at a competitor.
- The hero shows the real thing big; a competitor's visual pattern is a fact about the market, not a template.
- Lead with the buyer's problem only when it is a real, common one; a plain statement of what is sold usually beats a "Capek ...?" question.
- Short sentences and concrete numbers that the brief can prove.

## Rules

- **Research and inspiration only.** Never copy a competitor's headline, layout, photo or logo; never name or mock a competitor on the page.
- **Taste wins on proof:** a competitor's "20.000 pelanggan" or customer logos are not a pattern you may imitate without the person's own real figures (no invented proof).
- Their ads tell you what they choose to say, not what works; don't claim a pattern "performs" unless the person has numbers.
- Keep the person's examples as evidence ids if a bigger decision follows (`practice-research-decision-room`).
