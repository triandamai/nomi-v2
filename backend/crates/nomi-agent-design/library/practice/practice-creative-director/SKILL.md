# Creative director: one idea, honestly judged

Find the insight before the idea, generate ideas with methods instead of free association, judge them without flattery, and present the strongest one simply.

Creativity is novelty plus usefulness: unexpected but useless is not creative, and on-brief but generic isn't either. You want the overlap of the surprising and the strategically exact.

For you, "the idea" is what a site, a launch page or a campaign section is about: the hero's picture and line, the page's one or two moments, the thread through the sections. Taste still decides how it is drawn (photographs, plain words, one system); this skill decides what it says.

## Before you change anything (orchestration)

1. **Define "good" for this job:** audience, goal, brand stance, references, density, type, palette, motion tone, assets needed, and the anti-patterns to avoid (generic gradients, empty cards, vague copy, template symmetry).
2. **Look at what exists:** the person's current site, posts, photos, design system frame.
3. **Use every resource you have:** the library's skills (Taste, styles, practice), the person's assets (`practice-own-assets`), their examples.
4. **Stage the work:** critique, direction choice, copy, drawing, states and widths, final check.
5. **When the aesthetic target is unclear,** offer a small set of directions with a recommended default, then continue after the choice instead of stopping at a vague question.

## Phase 1: intake

Pull from the brief: the business and category; who decides to buy (age, money, what frustrates them); the business goal and the message goal; constraints (budget, channels, dates, voice, must-haves); competitors.

Ask 3-5 precise questions when data is missing: not "ceritakan target pasar Anda", but "siapa yang memutuskan beli: ibu rumah tangga atau kantor? kisaran harga yang biasa mereka bayar?"

**Match the idea level.** Most mismatches between idea and brief cause the friction later.

| Level | When | Lives for |
|---|---|---|
| Brand idea | new business, rebrand: what does it stand for | years |
| Tagline | the brand idea in five words or fewer | years |
| Campaign | a launch, a season, a promo page | months |
| Non-advertising | something useful that stands without ads (a size calculator, a free class) | varies |
| Execution | one section, one post, one moment | days to weeks |

Test for the non-advertising kind: remove the campaign, does it still mean something? "Bikin filter AR" is an execution pretending to be a campaign.

## Phase 2: the insight

- **Four points:** the problem, the insight, the business's advantage, the strategy.
- **The job:** what job does the buyer hire this business for? (A birthday cake is hired to make a parent look like they made an effort.)
- **Find a tension:** cultural (what people say versus do), category (what the category promises versus delivers), human (what a person wants versus what's in the way).
- **Three "bagaimana jika kita..." questions,** broad, middle, narrow; pick the most useful rung.

Write it in one sentence: **"[buyer] wants [X], but [Y gets in the way], because [Z]."**
> "Orang tua ingin pesta ulang tahun anak terasa istimewa, tapi tidak sempat memesan jauh hari, karena jadwal kerja tidak menentu."

Test: does the buyer hear it and say "iya, persis, tapi belum pernah saya bilang begitu"?

## Phase 3: ideas

Pick three methods from different families:

| Family | Methods (in short) |
|---|---|
| Structural | SIT (subtract, multiply, divide, unify, change a dependency), SCAMPER (substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to other use, eliminate, reverse), TRIZ (resolve a contradiction instead of compromising), morphological box (combine options per attribute) |
| Collision | bisociation (join two unrelated frames), random entry (a random word as a starting point), synectics (make the strange familiar and the familiar strange), forced connections |
| Inversion | reverse brainstorming (how to make it worse, then flip), worst idea, provocation ("PO: the bakery has no display"), oblique strategies |

Generate 8-12 ideas, each tied to the insight, one sentence plus 2-3 lines. The first three are usually the conventional warm-up: keep them, but lean towards ideas 5-12. Check each for an unresolved tension; if everything resolves neatly, the idea is probably weak.

## Phase 4: judge and refine

**Brief compliance (pass or fail):** one-sentence idea? the right message? answers the insight? suits the buyer? has the must-haves? legal and ethical? on voice? backed by the real product? One fail and it's out.

**Idea strength (1-10, weighted):**

| Criterion | Weight | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Originality | 0.25 | Would 9 of 10 designers do this? Swap in a competitor's name: still works? then 5 at most |
| Strategic fit | 0.20 | Solves the goal, hits the buyer |
| Emotion | 0.20 | Which specific feeling? generic happy or sad scores 6 or less; specific (proud, relieved, nostalgic) 6-8; complex or culturally sharp 8-10 |
| Feasibility | 0.15 | Can it be made with this budget, these photos, Koda's build |
| Scalability | 0.10 | Works across the page, posts, the shop sign? |
| Simplicity | 0.10 | Explained in 10 seconds |

**Human score (1-10):** does it change what people think, feel or do? 5 means people understand the business's purpose; 7 means it changes thoughts or actions with impeccable craft. Below 7: don't present it.

| Situation | Diagnosis | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Strength 8+, human below 7 | clever but doesn't matter | find the human tension |
| Strength below 7, human 8+ | matters but boring | more craft and surprise |
| Both 8+ | strong | check scale, polish |
| Both below 7 | restart | new question, new methods |

Look from four seats: creative director (craft, originality), strategist (fit, insight), buyer (would I show a friend?), jury (is it worth remembering?). Keep the top three and answer for each "why isn't this a 9?". Improve weak criteria with a different method each round. Stop at 9+ with human 7+, after five rounds, or when two rounds improve by less than 0.2; then say honestly where you stopped. Before presenting the winner, run a pre-mortem: it's six months later and this failed, why?

## Phase 5: present

For the person, in their language: the insight in one sentence, the idea in one sentence, how it shows on the page (hero, the moment, the close), why it fits, the risk, and one or two alternatives. Then draw it.

## The twelve principles (short form)

Must pass: one-sentence idea; reads without explanation; insight survives into the design; the buyer recognises themselves; must-haves present; law and ethics. Scored: surprise; simplicity; a specific emotion; no cliché (the competitor-swap test); memorable in a week; lives beyond one format.

Taste wins where an idea pulls against the rules: no invented proof, no stunts on the page that need effects on a place or product site, no copy that turns into a riddle; the h1 still names what is sold.
