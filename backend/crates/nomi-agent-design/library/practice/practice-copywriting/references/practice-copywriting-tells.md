# Copy tells beyond the word list

The shapes that make copy read as generated even after every banned word is gone, the second-generation tells that appear once copy has been de-slopped, how to build a writer before a full rewrite, and the self-check to run before you save. `taste-copy` holds the word lists, device budgets and Indonesian stock lines; this adds the structure.

Most of these are old human rhetoric. Generated copy gives itself away by using them by default, stacked, with nothing specific behind them. **Ban** = a tell even once. **Cap** = fine once, a tell when repeated or stacked.

Scopes: **short copy** is the hero, headings, buttons, banners, social captions; **section** is one hero or one page section. Button microcopy ("Tanpa kartu kredit") doesn't count toward caps.

## Structural patterns

| Pattern | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Contrast reveal** (Ban) | "Bukan sekadar toko roti. Ini rumah kedua Anda." "Bukan karena X. Karena Y." | State Y directly with its reason. Correct a wrong belief with the fact and evidence, not a staged denial |
| **Negation list** (Ban) | "Tanpa ribet, tanpa antre, tanpa biaya tambahan." | Say what does happen, in order: "Pesan lewat WhatsApp, ambil jam 10." State one real absence once, next to the button |
| **Trailing pile-on** (Ban) | "Laporan langsung dari data Anda, tanpa ekspor, tanpa spreadsheet, tanpa salinan kedua." | End the sentence at the claim. A clause that adds a new fact may stay; restatements go |
| **Self-answered question** (Ban) | "Hasilnya? Antrean 3x lebih cepat." "Kenapa ini penting? Karena..." | Delete the question, keep the answer. FAQ questions and a question in the reader's own voice ("Perlu kue besok pagi?") are fine |
| **Colon reveal** (Ban) | "Rahasianya: konsistensi." "Satu kata: cepat." | A normal sentence. Colons for lists, labels and quotes |
| **Fragment drumbeat** (Cap 1 a section) | "Simpel. Cepat. Beres." | Merge into one sentence that carries a fact |
| **Reflexive threes** (Cap 1 a section) | "Cepat, aman, dan terjangkau." | As many items as the facts support; often the single strongest fact: "Kirim ke seluruh Yogyakarta di hari yang sama" |
| **Em dashes** (Ban in short copy; 1-2 a page in long copy) | "Bukan alat — tapi rekan kerja" | Full stop, comma, colon or brackets |
| **Uniform rhythm** (Cap) | Every sentence 12-18 words, every section the same length | Vary on purpose; let a section be two lines if that's all it needs |
| **Dodging "adalah/punya"** (Cap) | "Hadir sebagai pusat...", "Menjadi bukti...", "Memiliki ragam..." | Use "adalah", "punya", or the real verb: "Mencatat setiap pesanan, tanggal ambil dan pembayaran" |
| **Abstract subjects** (Cap) | "Platform kami memberdayakan tim untuk..." | Make the customer the subject: "Kasir Anda menutup kas dalam lima menit" |
| **False ranges** (Cap) | "Dari UMKM hingga korporasi." "Dari pesanan hingga pengiriman." | Name the segment you serve or the steps you cover |
| **Synonym cycling** (Cap) | The same thing called aplikasi, platform, sistem and dasbor on one page | Pick the customer's word and repeat it |

## Openers, closers, setups (Ban)

| Pattern | Example | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Era opener | "Di era digital seperti sekarang..." | Open on the customer's problem or the fact |
| False-choice opener | "Baik Anda pemilik warung maupun restoran besar..." | Name who the page is for |
| "Bayangkan..." | "Bayangkan tidak perlu lagi menagih utang pelanggan." | Show the real before and after |
| Throat-clearing | "Faktanya:", "Jujur saja,", "Inilah yang tidak diketahui banyak orang" | Cut it; if the claim can't stand alone it needs evidence |
| Announcing | "Di bawah ini Anda akan menemukan..." | Start with the content |
| Fake-profound kicker | "Karena bisnis Anda layak mendapat yang terbaik." | End on a concrete sentence, a number, the offer or the button |
| Filler transitions | "Perlu dicatat", "Selain itu", "Yang tak kalah penting" | Delete; the sentence works without it |

## Hedges, puffery, weasels

- **Hedge stacks** (Cap): "dapat membantu Anda berpotensi mengurangi..." State what you know or scope the claim.
- **Importance puffery** (Ban): "menandai babak baru", "mendefinisikan ulang kategori". State the fact.
- **Weasel sources** (Ban): "para ahli sepakat", "studi menunjukkan", "dipercaya para pelaku industri". Name the source or cut it.
- **Invented labels** (Cap): "pajak integrasi", "jebakan pertumbuhan". Coin a term only if the page defines and reuses it.
- **Hype punctuation** (Ban): "Siap naik level?!"; no exclamation marks in body copy.

## Formatting tells

- Emoji as bullets or headings (Ban). Unicode arrows and fancy letters in body copy (Ban).
- A bold label on every bullet (Cap): keep that for spec tables.
- Every bullet opening the same way, "Memastikan... / Memberikan... / Memungkinkan..." (Cap): open on the customer outcome and vary the grammar.
- Title Case Everywhere (Cap; Taste already sets sentence case).
- Label headings "Apa / Kenapa / Bagaimana" (Cap): write headings that name a thing or make a claim.

## Second-generation tells

These appear in copy that is already clean of the patterns above. Check for them last.

| Tell | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Wisdom-shaped sentences** (Ban) | "Alat yang baik tidak menghalangi." "Kecepatan adalah fitur." | Replace with the specific fact it gestures at, or cut |
| **Performed cleverness** (Cap) | "Kotak masuk Anda, minus kebisingan." | Keep one only if the brand is playful and it says something true |
| **Staccato bursts** (Cap) | "Cepat. Mudah. Dan langsung jalan." | Merge into a sentence with a fact |
| **Perfect balance** (Cap) | Every section: heading, two sentences, three bullets | Let the strongest section run long and the weakest shrink or go |
| **Omniscient casual** (Cap) | Relaxed voice that knows every answer, admits no limit | Scope claims; say who it isn't for ("Belum cocok untuk lebih dari 3 cabang") |
| **Self-narration** (Ban) | "Inilah kenapa ini penting." "Di sinilah menariknya." | Delete; add a detail if the point needs help |
| **Tidy closers** (Ban) | "Karena pelanggan Anda layak lebih." "Masa depan kasir dimulai hari ini." | End on a number, a named product, the offer or the button |
| **Reflexive specificity** (Cap) | Every claim has a suspiciously neat stat or anecdote | Every number and story must come from the brief; otherwise list it as a NEED |

## Build a writer (before a full rewrite in situation A)

Answer silently:
1. **Who is writing?** The owner, the head baker, the developer who built it, and why they are writing this page.
2. **What do they know cold?** That part gets the most words and the most specific detail.
3. **What bores them or what don't they know?** Compress it or cut it.
4. **What is their honest, arguable opinion** about the trade or the old way? ("Kebanyakan aplikasi kasir punya 40 menu. Kami punya enam.")
5. **If they had to cut a third, what goes first?** Cut it now.

What this produces: uneven density (the section the writer cares about runs longest), mixed register (one exact trade term, then plain words), honest limits, lopsided structure, sentence length that follows the thought, opinions that cost something, first person only when it carries a fact.

Rules over the persona: **never invent experience** (no made-up "waktu saya buka warung pertama..." story, no invented customer quote: use what the brief gives or list a NEED); in situation B the writer already exists, so match them; keep the brand's register (a clinic stays formal; uneven density and real opinions work in formal voice too).

## Rewrite rules

1. Rewrite from the facts (the mechanism, the number, the product, the step), never by synonym swaps, which create new tells.
2. Every claim sentence needs a number, a name, a mechanism or a concrete action.
3. Swap test on the h1, the sub-line and every heading.
4. Two or more Cap patterns in one paragraph: rewrite the paragraph.
5. Don't invent to de-slop: replacing "terbaik" with a made-up statistic is worse.
6. Choose devices on purpose; the same device on every line is slop.
7. Vary the fixes: if every reveal becomes ", karena..." that is the new tic.
8. If no h1 passes the swap test with what you have, write the best one and ask for the differentiator.

## The self-check (before every save)

1. Search short copy for "bukan", "tanpa", "?", ":" and "—"; check each hit against the bans above.
2. Read every sentence over 20 words; more than two commas means look for a pile-on.
3. Count fragments, threes and capped words per section.
4. Read it aloud as the owner. Mark anywhere it sounds like a press release or a LinkedIn post.
5. Swap test on the h1, sub-line and headings.
6. Check the last line of every section for a tidy closer, look for wisdom-shaped sentences, compare section lengths.

If a fix needs a fact you don't have, ask for it instead of writing around it.
